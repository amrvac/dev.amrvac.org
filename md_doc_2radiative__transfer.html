Radiative transfer with short characteristics in MPI-AMRVAC

Optically thin cooling describes radiation that escapes freely, which fails in the solar photosphere, in the dense chromosphere and in the cores of prominence threads, where the gas absorbs much of what it emits. For these layers MPI-AMRVAC solves the radiative transfer equation in gray LTE with the method of short characteristics (SC), and adds the resulting radiative heating or cooling to the energy equation. The module works in 2D Cartesian geometry, on a uniform grid within the layers where it is active. This page describes the physics, how to use the module, and how it is organised. Set the normalization units in usr_init(), since the opacities and the radiation constants are converted with them.

Physics

Along every ray direction \(\boldsymbol\mu\) the specific intensity \(I\) obeys

\[ \boldsymbol\mu\cdot\nabla I=\chi\,(B-I), \]

where \(\chi=\kappa\rho\) is the opacity per unit length and the source function is the Planck function, written as an energy density \(B=aT^4\). Scattering is not included, so for a given state the intensity of each ray follows from one sweep across the grid. The rays sample the unit sphere with a fixed quadrature, and their weighted sums give the mean intensity \(J\) and the radiative flux \(\boldsymbol F=c\sum_k w_k\boldsymbol\mu_k I_k\).

The heating per unit volume is

\[ Q=c\,\chi\,(J-B). \]

Near the top of a layer this form is accurate. Deep down, where \(J\) and \(B\) become nearly equal, their difference is dominated by round-off, and the equivalent conservative form \(Q=-\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol F\) is better. The module therefore blends the two,

\[ Q=w\,c\,\chi\,(J-B)+(1-w)(-\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol F), \qquad w=e^{-k\tau}, \]

with \(\tau\) the optical depth measured from the top of the layer and \(k\) set by rt_sc_blend_k. Where the column above a cell is optically thin, the heating is tapered away by the factor \(\tau^2/(\tau^2+\tau_{\min}^2)\), since the optically thin cooling describes those cells.

Opacity and the hand-over to thin cooling

The opacity is one of three models chosen by rt_sc_opacity: a tabulated Rosseland mean, an analytic fit of the H-minus and Kramers opacities with electron scattering, or a routine supplied by the user. The two built-in models switch the transfer off smoothly in the hot, tenuous corona. A cell keeps its transfer when it is cooler than rt_sc_Tcap or denser than the pressure rt_sc_p_TR, and outside both \(\chi\) and \(B\) are scaled down together, so that the corona becomes both transparent and dark to the transfer.

Where the transfer is active, the radiative losses are divided between the SC heating and the optically thin cooling, so that no loss is counted twice. The share of the thin cooling is

\[ w_{\rm thin}=\tfrac12\left[1+\tanh\frac{T-T_0}{\Delta T}\right] \max\left[0,\,1-(p/p_{\rm TR})^2\right] \]

inside an RT layer and one outside, and the SC heating is multiplied by \(1-w_{\rm thin}\). The radiative cooling module applies this weight to its own losses automatically.

Practical use

Activating the module

The transfer is switched on in the physics namelist, in the same way as radiative cooling, 

&hd_list
   hd_radiative_transfer = .true.
/

or with mhd_radiative_transfer = .true. in &mhd_list. Both need the energy equation. The settings of the transfer are read from &rt_list, described below, and the module then sets itself up. It registers the output variables Qrad, the heating, and Frad, the vertical flux, which are written to the data files with the other variables. It solves the transfer once per time step, before the time integration, and adds the heating to the energy equation at every stage, limited so that one stage changes the gas energy by at most rt_sc_cfl of itself. When radiative cooling is also active, the heating takes only its share \(1-w_{\rm thin}\), and the refinement routines hold every block of a band on one AMR level. Nothing needs to be added to the user file.

Two hooks remain for setups that need them. With rt_sc_opacity = 'usr', the user points usr_rt_sc_chiB at a routine in usr_init() that returns \(\chi\) and \(B\) on a block, as the test tests/hd/sc_rt_2D does. A setup that controls its energy input from the radiative output can call rt_sc_emergent_tau1, described below.

Emergent flux

A setup that controls its energy input from the radiative output, for example through the entropy of the inflow at the bottom of a convection simulation, needs the flux that leaves the photosphere. rt_sc_emergent_tau1 computes the horizontal mean of the vertical flux on the surface \(\tau=1\) of the first band and stores it in rt_sc_Ftau1. The flux at the top of the band itself is not suitable, because the radiative flux keeps growing above \(\tau=1\).

Numerics and implementation

Transport. Each ray is integrated with the linear short characteristics of Olson and Kunasz. A cell takes the intensity at the upwind point on the face the ray crosses first, interpolated linearly along that face together with \(\chi\) and \(B\), and integrates across one step with the source varying linearly in optical depth. The rays are swept cell by cell from the upwind corner, so every cell is visited once per direction.

Solvers. The default solver is a pipelined wavefront (KBA). Each rank sweeps only its own blocks of a band, one diagonal of blocks at a time, and passes the intensity on through the ghost cells, so no rank ever holds the whole band. The rays that share an upwind corner follow one another through the wavefront, and the corners run together. The ray-by-ray wavefront (rt_sc_pipeline=.false.) sends one ray at a time and gives the same result bit for bit. The replicated solver (rt_sc_distributed=.false.) is the original implementation, kept for comparison and not for production runs. It assembles the whole band on every rank, and its periodic wrap is not exact: a ray swept column by column reaches the seam before its upwind column holds that ray, so the seam takes a vacuum inflow for it. The wavefront carries the intensity across the seam from the previous solve instead, which is exact at steady state.

Bands and AMR. The transfer assumes uniform cells, so the refinement routines hold every block that overlaps a band on rt_sc_amr_level, refining coarser blocks and coarsening finer ones. Outside the bands the mesh refines freely, and the heating there is zero.

Modules. The code is in src/physics. mod_rt_sc_slab is the module that user files and the rest of the code use. It holds the coupling to the gas, the heating source, the thin-cooling share, the AMR hold and the emergent flux. mod_rt_sc_state reads and converts the parameters and holds the shared state, mod_rt_sc_solvers the three solvers, mod_rt_sc_kernel the quadrature, the sweep and the heating formula, and mod_rt_sc_opacity the opacity models.

Limitations. The module is 2D and gray, with LTE source functions and no scattering. It assumes uniform Cartesian cells: other coordinate systems are refused, stretched grids are not supported, and each band lies on a single AMR level. In the wavefront the corner ghost of a block can hold the next ray, so the corner value is rebuilt from the two face neighbours. In the slab test this costs up to 8.5% of the peak heating in the corner cells of a block.

Tests. tests/hd/sc_rt_2D checks the transport of a beam across the periodic seam, the heating blend and taper on an absorbing slab, and the Rosseland opacity on a stratified atmosphere, with the replicated, ray-by-ray and pipelined solvers.
